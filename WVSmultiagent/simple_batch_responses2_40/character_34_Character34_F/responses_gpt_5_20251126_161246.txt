What I’d favor
- Employment-based, tied to real demand:
  - Use independent data to set sector/region targets where shortages are proven.
  - Require prevailing wages, transparent postings, and good‑faith local recruitment first.
  - Make work visas portable so people can leave bad employers; that’s how you stop undercutting.
- Numerical limits that flex:
  - Set annual ranges by category (not one blunt cap), with formulas that adjust for unemployment in the occupation, housing vacancy, and school/clinic capacity.
  - Publish the dials so it’s predictable and less political whiplash.
- Humanitarian lanes, protected:
  - Keep a refugee floor that doesn’t shrink in a downturn, with surge capacity for crises.
  - For asylum, speed up processing and work authorization so people aren’t pushed into the shadow economy.
- Family reunification, faster and fair:
  - Keep core family categories with reasonable caps, kill the years‑long backlogs, and crack down on scams.
- Seasonal/essential programs with teeth:
  - Modernize guest worker programs in ag, care, and tourism: employer pays for decent housing/transport, no recruitment fees, joint liability up the contracting chain, real oversight.
  - Offer an earned path to longer‑term status after multiple seasons with clean records so we’re not cycling people forever.
- Enforcement where it matters:
  - High penalties for wage theft, misclassification, and illegal retaliation—applied to prime contractors, not just the smallest subcontractor.
  - Phase in E‑Verify tied to stronger worker protections and easy status checks so mistakes don’t cost someone their livelihood.
- Integration and local support:
  - Federal dollars follow arrivals for schools, clinics, ESL, and housing in host communities; employers that benefit pay into this.
  - Fund credential recognition and apprenticeships so skills aren’t wasted and locals can move up too.
- An earned fix for those already here:
  - For long‑term, law‑abiding undocumented neighbors: background checks, fines/taxes, English/civics, then a real line to get right with the system. It’s better for everyone than permanent limbo.

Why this mix
- Open borders isn’t fair to working people when housing and schools are already tight; a total shut door isn’t who we are and just fuels abuse.
- Job‑demand with portable visas and prevailing wages fills real gaps without turning workers into leverage.
- Flexible caps and local investment keep social friction down and make the rules feel legitimate.
- Clear lanes—work, family, refuge—reduce chaos at the border and undercut the shadow market.

That’s the balance I can stand behind: orderly, humane, and honest about capacity—one floor for wages and safety, with enough flexibility to meet real needs and enough support so neighbors don’t feel squeezed.
