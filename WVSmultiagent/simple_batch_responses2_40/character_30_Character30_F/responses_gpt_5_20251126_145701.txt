  - Seasonal visas where the work truly is seasonal (harvests, tourism), with clear start/finish dates.

- Set flexible numerical limits, not a hard cap carved in stone:

- Keep a strong humanitarian lane:

- Family reunification with responsibility:

- Pair admissions with investment and enforcement:
  - Fund local schools, clinics, and affordable housing where newcomers settle. If the federal government sets policy, it should send dollars, not just good wishes.
  - Crack down on wage theft and cash‑under‑the‑table schemes. Fine and bar abusive employers from hiring visas in the future.
  - Simple, humane border and screening—orderly legal channels, quick decisions on asylum claims, and fair removals when claims don’t meet the mark.

- Build ladders for locals:
  - Apprenticeships, short courses, and on‑the‑job training so hometown folks can step into openings and move up. Give locals a real first shot, not a token posting.

- A path to stay for those who meet the mark:

- Only numbers without looking at demand is clumsy—you end up with people in the wrong places or black‑market hiring.

This kind of policy feels like the way we run a good union hall: honest lines, clear rules, proof before privilege, and help for the whole crew so the work gets done safely. It protects working people from being played against each other, welcomes newcomers who play by the rules, and gives communities the support they need to make belonging real.
