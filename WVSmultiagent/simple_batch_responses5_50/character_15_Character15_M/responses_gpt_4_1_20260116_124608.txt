J (扮演Character15_M) 回答: (as Character15_M)

If I’m being true to myself as Character15_M, I wouldn’t pick either extreme—total openness or total refusal—because real life isn’t black and white like that. Here’s my thinking:

- **Job Demand:** Matching immigration to actual labor market needs makes sense. If businesses truly can’t find workers locally—whether that’s in tech, agriculture, care work, or whatever—then letting in migrants to fill those gaps helps the economy and keeps things running. But I’d want to see real evidence that those jobs can’t be filled locally, and I think employers should be required to offer good wages and conditions to everyone, local or migrant, to avoid exploitation and a “race to the bottom.”

- **Numerical Limits:** I don’t see anything wrong with reasonable caps, updated each year based on economic and social data. That way, we can manage resources and integration support—schools, housing, public health—without causing resentment or overload. It also gives the public confidence that immigration is being managed thoughtfully, not just left open-ended.

- **Humanitarian & Family Reasons:** There should always be room for refugees and those genuinely fleeing danger, and for people to reunite with close family. That’s a basic moral commitment for a country like the US. But I’d also say we need strong support systems to help these groups integrate, not just bring people in and leave them to figure it out.

- **Social Integration:** Policy is only half the story. The government needs to fund programs that support language learning, job training, and cultural exchange for both newcomers and locals. Integration is a two-way street—new arrivals should make an effort to participate, and host communities should be open to change and diversity.

So, the balance for me is: job-based admissions with real safeguards, a solid humanitarian component, annual limits to keep things manageable, and real investment in social integration. That’s not perfect, but it’s the fairest approach I can see—one that recognizes economic realities, keeps the country open to those in need, and tries to maintain social cohesion and public trust.
