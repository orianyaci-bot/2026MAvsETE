J (扮演Character38_M) 回答: (as Character38_M)

I’d steer clear of the extremes. “Let anyone come” ignores capacity and public trust; “let no one in” hurts our economy and betrays commitments we’ve made. I’d build a hybrid that is generous where we have room and need, firm where we must be, and predictable everywhere.

What that looks like:

- Employment-based channels tied to real demand
  - An independent labor market board sets sector targets and adjusts quarterly based on wages, vacancies, and unemployment.
  - Salary floors at or above the local median (or a high percentile) for the role, plus proof of good-faith local recruitment.
  - Visa portability with a grace period so workers can change employers—no indenture. Strong penalties for wage theft and retaliation.
  - Employer obligations: contribute to a training fund, report pay and conditions, and accept audits. If you benefit from global talent, you help train local talent too.

- Numerical limits with feedback
  - A clear overall ceiling with bands for employment, family, and humanitarian streams, updated to track capacity and labor indicators.
  - “Circuit breakers”: if sectoral wages stall or unemployment spikes, slow new entries in that category until conditions normalize.

- Humanitarian commitments done responsibly
  - Set refugee/asylum targets tied to processing and resettlement capacity. Speed adjudication; give work authorization quickly so people can support themselves.
  - Use community/private sponsorship and fund host communities—schools, ESL, clinics, housing—so the welcome is real.

- Family reunification with predictability
  - Prioritize nuclear family, clear the backlogs, and set transparent timetables. Maintain limited, capped categories for extended family with points for self-sufficiency.

- Seasonal and essential work without exploitation
  - Modernize H-2A/H-2B: enforce wage floors, safe housing, and real oversight; allow workers to move among vetted employers within the season; hold labor contractors jointly liable.
  - Where possible, pair visas with productivity investments (training, mechanization) so we’re not just locking in low-road models.

- Integration as policy, not charity
  - Invest in ESL, credential recognition, apprenticeships, and childcare/transport so locals and newcomers can actually take available jobs.
  - Create community impact funds financed by modest visa fees to expand school capacity, public health, and affordable housing in fast-changing areas.

- Orderly borders and credible enforcement
  - Manage the border with due process and consequence: rapid, fair asylum screening; lawful pathways that reduce irregular crossings; swift return for those who don’t qualify.
  - Shift interior enforcement toward bad actors—traffickers, wage thieves—rather than status alone, to protect labor standards.

- Earned permanence and a clean slate going forward
  - A clear, earned path to permanence for long-settled, law-abiding people who work, pay taxes, and learn English. Gray zones invite abuse.
  - Pair that with modernized employment verification and real legal channels so future flows are above-board.

Why this balances fairness and social integration:
- It keeps competition on the level—wage floors, portability, and enforcement stop a race to the bottom and protect both local and migrant workers.
- It aligns entries with capacity—labor indicators and community funding keep services from being swamped.
- It honors humanitarian obligations without letting systems drown—faster decisions, work access, and support for hosts.
- It gives the public a dashboard and levers—numbers move with facts, not slogans.

In short: make the circle wider where it serves our values and interests, and make the rules sturdier so people can trust the system. That’s the balance I can defend.
