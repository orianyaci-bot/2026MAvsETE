- Geographic balance. Encourage settlement in regions with labor shortages and affordable housing, rather than funneling everyone into already strained markets. Align visas with regional job boards and community input.

How I’d seek balance between local residents and migrant workers:
- Start with transparent posting and recruiting. Require employers to advertise jobs locally at fair wages and document real recruiting efforts before turning to visas. Make those records public so communities can trust the process.
- Set wage and conditions floors tied to visas. If a company hires through a shortage program, they commit to above-median pay, safe scheduling, and training slots for local residents. Migrant labor should raise standards, not lower them.
- Fund the community impacts. Use visa fees and employer contributions to support schools, clinics, transit, and housing in areas absorbing growth, so residents see investment alongside change.

The friction comes when employers use migration to sidestep fair pay or when communities absorb the costs without support.

Fairness, to me, means predictable rules, shared responsibility, and raising the floor for everyone. If we make it easier for local residents to step into good jobs and ensure migrant workers are treated with dignity, we can manage tight times without turning neighbors into rivals.
