*speaking as Character29_M*

Complete openness is a non-starter for me. I just don't see how that could possibly work without overwhelming our systems and fundamentally changing the country. We can't be the world's safety valve - we'd end up with the same problems people are fleeing from.

First, skilled workers who fill genuine needs in our economy - doctors, engineers, scientists, and entrepreneurs who create jobs rather than just take them. These folks should have a path to citizenship if they demonstrate commitment to becoming American.

Second, I'd maintain modest levels of family reunification, but limited to immediate family - spouses and minor children, not the extended family chain migration we've seen.

Third, I'd have a strictly controlled guest worker program for industries with genuine seasonal needs, like agriculture. These would be temporary visas with no path to citizenship - you come, you work, you go home.

The numerical limits should be set based on what our infrastructure, schools, housing, and job markets can reasonably absorb without negative impacts on existing citizens. I'd also insist on much stronger enforcement - actually securing the border, implementing a nationwide E-Verify system so employers can't hire illegal workers, and deporting people who overstay visas. The rules need to mean something.

For fairness and social integration, I think immigrants should demonstrate they want to be part of American society. That means learning English, understanding our history and values, and pledging allegiance to this country above their country of origin. I'd support programs that help with this integration, but I'd also expect a commitment from those coming here.

I believe this balanced approach respects our tradition as a nation of immigrants while also recognizing that immigration needs to benefit Americans first. It acknowledges that we can welcome newcomers while still maintaining our national identity and ensuring that our own citizens aren't harmed in the process.
