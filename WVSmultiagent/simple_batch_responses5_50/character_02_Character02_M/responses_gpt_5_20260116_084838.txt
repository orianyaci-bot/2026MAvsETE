I’m not for “let everyone in tomorrow,” and I’m not for “shut the door.” Both feel lazy. The sweet spot is clear paths in, real worker power, and support for the places that do the welcoming. My mix:

- A strong floor for everyone
  - Same wages, safety, and rights no matter your passport. Real penalties for wage theft, joint liability up the subcontractor chain, and the right to change jobs and organize without losing status.

- Numbers, but flexible and honest
  - Set annual ranges, not hard caps, with mid‑year adjustments based on real indicators: unemployment, vacancies, wage growth, housing capacity. Publish the math so people can see why numbers move.

- Labor pathways tied to need, without indenture
  - Short‑term/seasonal visas when an independent board confirms shortages; require prevailing wages + benefits. No recruitment fees. Full portability—if a boss is abusive, you can walk.
  - For longer‑term skills, allow points/wage‑based visas but don’t trap people in one employer. Keep a path to permanence so we don’t build a permanent second class.

- Keep family and humanitarian doors open
  - Family reunification stays core—that’s how stability happens.
  - Predictable refugee targets with proper support, and faster, fairer asylum decisions so people aren’t stuck in limbo. Work authorization while cases move.

- Earned status for people already here
  - Dreamers, long‑time undocumented with clean records, farm and essential workers: background checks, taxes, English/civics classes—then a real path to green cards. Bringing folks above ground cuts exploitation that drags everyone down.

- Match people to places and fund the basics
  - Incentives to settle where jobs and housing exist; federal per‑arrival dollars for schools, clinics, transit, and ESL in receiving communities. Don’t stick cities with the bill.

- Invest in mobility for locals and newcomers
  - Paid apprenticeships, community‑college certs, credential recognition for immigrants, ESL with childcare, and wraparound supports. The point is to expand the pie, not reshuffle the same slice.

- Smarter border and enforcement
  - Modern ports of entry, more legal channels to reduce chaos, go after smugglers and abusive labor brokers. Focus enforcement on employer abuse, not tearing up families. Any work verification tool must have anti‑discrimination guardrails.

Why not the extremes:
- Complete openness: sounds kind, but housing, schools, and job training aren’t infinite. You get backlash and chaos; the most vulnerable pay first.
- Job‑demand only: people aren’t just “workers.” You need family unity and humanitarian lanes, or you create churn and pain.
- Strict numerical limits with no flex: ignores real‑world swings and fuels illegal hiring when caps are out of step.
- Refusing entry: we’re an aging country; care, ag, and construction don’t run without immigrants. It also betrays our values and pushes people into the shadows, where abuse spreads.

What success looks like to me:
- Fewer “ghost” workers and more W‑2s.
- Wages at the bottom inching up, not down.
- Faster asylum timelines, fewer people stuck in shelters.
- Employers competing on safety and pay, not on who they can exploit.
- Neighborhoods getting funding when population rises, so residents—old and new—feel the benefit.

That balance—clear doors, strong floors, real support—is how you make fairness and belonging more than slogans. It’s also how you stop bosses from playing us against each other.
