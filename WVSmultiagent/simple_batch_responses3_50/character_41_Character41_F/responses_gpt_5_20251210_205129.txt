- Job‑demand admissions with teeth: If there’s a real shortage, bring people in—but only with equal pay, safety, and the right to change employers so they aren’t trapped. - Separate tracks for family and humanitarian cases: Family unity and asylum shouldn’t be squeezed into labor slots. Keep steady numbers for reunification and a functioning, humane asylum system with work authorization and integration support, so people can stand on their own feet.
- Status for people already here: A real, earned path for long‑settled neighbors—background checks, taxes, English/civics classes if needed—so they can step out of the shadows, join unions, and stop being used to push wages down.
- Invest where people land: Tie visas and resettlement to funding for housing, schools, clinics, and transit. Give grants to the cities and towns doing the welcoming so locals don’t feel abandoned and newcomers don’t get blamed for shortages they didn’t create.

This mix feels fair to me because it centers equal treatment at work, plans for real capacity, protects families and refugees, and gives both locals and newcomers a shot at better.
