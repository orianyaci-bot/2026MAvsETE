- Real pathways for people who are already here and settled. Keeping long-time neighbors in the shadows just hands bad bosses a tool to scare them quiet. Give a way to get right—background check, fees, learning the basics—so we’re all playing by the same rules.
- Back up communities that grow. When a town takes in more people (from inside the country or abroad), money should follow the headcount: schools, clinics, transit. Fund it with employer fees or visa surcharges so existing residents aren’t left holding the bag.

How to balance locals and migrant workers when jobs are tight
- Throttle by need. Set sector caps that move with unemployment. If construction is soft in our county, slow new permits there; if elder care can’t hire enough aides even after training locals, open more slots in that field.
- Priority for public jobs. On city or state contracts, require local hiring targets and apprenticeship ratios. That helps young people get a leg up during lean times.
- Invest in skills that match the real openings. Free or low-cost training for CNA, HVAC, CDL, welding—stuff that actually pays. Tie it to guaranteed interviews with employers before they look elsewhere.

- I’ve lost shifts to budget cuts and seen whole crews replaced through temp agencies. It wasn’t because the new guys were “bad”—it was because the rules let the company do it cheap. When the rules are clear and fair, the undercutting stops.
- When we set a solid floor and stick to it, diversity isn’t a threat. It’s more hands to build with. When we let the floor crumble, everyone scrambles, and resentment takes root.

Bottom line: fairness is one line, one floor, one set of rules. Start by giving locals a real shot—post, train, pay right. What I’d support

- Humanitarian track: Keep room for refugees and people fleeing persecution, with thorough but fast screening and real resettlement support so families aren’t stuck in limbo.
- Family reunification with guardrails: Prioritize spouses/partners and minor kids, keep timelines predictable, and don’t make folks choose between work and family.
- Pathway for long‑settled undocumented neighbors: Background checks, taxes, fees scaled to income, basic civics/English—so we stop handing bad bosses leverage and get everyone under the same rules.

- Hard caps with no link to reality: Numbers divorced from actual labor conditions create shortages in some places and resentment in others. Make caps move with data.

How this balances fairness and integration
- Fair shot for locals: Posting and training first, with decent wages, so people here aren’t bypassed.
- Fair rules for everyone working: One floor—pay, safety, voice—so no one is used to undercut anyone else.

I come at this as someone who’s watched bosses game the system and neighbors carry the load.
