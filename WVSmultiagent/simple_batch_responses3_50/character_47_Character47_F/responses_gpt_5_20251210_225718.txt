- Adjustable numerical limits: Set national and regional caps that match capacity—housing, schools, clinics—reviewed yearly. Don’t use a blunt one-size number; tune by sector (eldercare vs. construction) and region.
- Humanitarian commitments: Keep a clear, funded pathway for refugees and asylum seekers—fast processing, solid vetting, and real resettlement support so towns aren’t left scrambling.

- Pure numbers only: A blunt cap ignores local realities and can starve places that need help while flooding others.
- Pure job-demand with no limits: Too easy to game by posting low wages and calling it a shortage. Needs proof and ceilings.

- Fairness: Same rules for everyone, floors high enough to stop a race to the bottom, and accountability aimed at employers who cheat, not just workers trying to survive.
- Belonging: Bring people in at a pace communities can absorb, with language access and civic orientation, so newcomers join the daily life, not just the labor pool.
- Predictability: Clear numbers, clear channels, regular reviews. People plan their lives better when they know the rules won’t whiplash.

If we do those pieces together—proof-based job admissions, caps that match capacity, real integration support, and strong labor standards—we protect the folks already here, welcome the help we actually need, and keep dignity at the center. That’s the balance I trust.
