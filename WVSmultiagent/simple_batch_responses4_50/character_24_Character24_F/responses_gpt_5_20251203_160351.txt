- Keep pathways open where we truly need hands, with equal rights on the job.
- Make sure the people already living here—whatever their origin—aren’t shut out by opaque hiring or lack of training.
- Scale public services to growth so scarcity doesn’t turn neighbors into rivals.

I wouldn’t pick “open the gates” or “shut them tight.” I’d go with a managed, fair system that matches real needs, protects wages, and keeps communities from getting overwhelmed. Something like this:

- Admit based on verified job demand, with equal pay and protections. If an employer says they need people, they should prove they’ve posted widely, offered fair wages, safe conditions, and decent schedules. - Set adjustable numerical limits tied to the economy and capacity. Have an annual ceiling with room to flex by sector and region, based on unemployment, vacancy rates, and service capacity (schools, clinics, housing). When the “temperature” cools—high unemployment—slow admissions for non-shortage roles. When it heats up—real shortages—open the valve. - Keep a humanitarian floor for refugees and asylum separate from labor numbers. Honor due process, do smart vetting, and invest in case management, housing, and language classes so folks can stabilize and contribute. Being humane shouldn’t depend on this week’s job report.

- Enforce workplace standards like it actually matters. Equal pay for equal work, real penalties for wage theft and misclassification, and protection for whistleblowers. If everyone plays by the same rules, there’s less resentment and less incentive to undercut.

- Create a realistic path to stability for long-term, law-abiding workers. If someone’s been here working, paying taxes, and raising kids, give them a way out of the shadows. It’s better for families, better for enforcement, and better for the wage floor.

- Keep borders and vetting smart and humane. Go hard after trafficking and criminal networks; don’t make regular families collateral damage. Make legal channels clear and efficient so people aren’t pushed into risky routes.

Open borders ignore capacity; hard bans ignore reality and fairness. Matching admissions to real demand, holding employers to the same standards for everyone, and building up community services keeps the floor from dropping and lets newcomers integrate without chaos.

That’s the balance that feels fair to me: welcome where we need the hands, dignity and equal rules on the job, steady support for the people already living here, and planning so growth doesn’t break the basics we all rely on.
