When jobs are tight, fairness to me means two things at once: you don’t let people already here get pushed to the edge, and you don’t turn newcomers into scapegoats or disposable labor. Clear rules, steady expectations, and some shared sacrifice—that’s the frame.

A few practical balances I’d look for:

- Make employers prove real need. Before bringing in outside workers, post the jobs at fair wages, offer decent hours and conditions, and recruit locally. If no one bites, that tells you it’s a genuine shortage, not a race to the bottom.
- Guard the floor, not just the door. Set and enforce wage and safety standards so no one—local or migrant—can be undercut. When standards are strong, cheaper labor stops being the shortcut, and the pressure to pit groups against each other eases.

- Share the community load. If a neighborhood sees rapid inflows, set aside money for schools, clinics, and housing so the basics don’t get stretched to breaking. That keeps resentment from building and helps everyone settle in.

For migrant workers, fairness means dignity: clear pathways, decent conditions, and the chance to belong if they follow the rules and contribute. For local residents, fairness means they aren’t sidelined, and that the community they’ve built isn’t treated like a revolving door.
